\subsection{Risk Analysis}

\subsubsection{General}

Risk analysis for the \textbf{BN-100 LUX} Infant Radiant Warmer is addressed
through the manufacturer's risk management documentation.

The available Risk Management Report identifies hazards and hazardous
situations associated with the design, manufacture and use of the device and
identifies corresponding risk-control measures.

The risk analysis is considered in relation to the intended use of the
device, its operating principles, its electrical and thermal functions, its
temperature monitoring system and its mechanical structure.

\subsubsection{Risk Management Basis}

The available manufacturer risk management documentation is identified as:

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Risk Management Report (BN-100/BN-100A)};

    \item Document No.: \textbf{YF-7.3-24-02 (w)};

    \item Version: \textbf{A/0}.

\end{itemize}

The document identifies risks associated with the medical device and
provides documented risk-control measures.

The reference to BN-100/BN-100A corresponds to the title of the available
manufacturer risk management document. It is used here as documentary
evidence and does not change the identification of the device subject to
this dossier, which is the \textbf{BN-100 LUX}.

\subsubsection{Identification of Hazards}

The manufacturer risk management documentation considers hazards associated
with several functional and component groups of the device.

These include, in particular:

\begin{itemize}

    \item electrical hazards;

    \item thermal hazards;

    \item temperature-control hazards;

    \item temperature-sensor failures;

    \item alarm-system failures;

    \item mechanical hazards;

    \item hazards associated with patient-contact components;

    \item hazards associated with component or system failures;

    \item hazards associated with manufacturing and assembly processes.

\end{itemize}

The identified hazards are considered in relation to the intended use and
reasonably foreseeable conditions of use of the device.

\subsubsection{Electrical Hazards}

The risk management documentation identifies electrical components and
circuits relevant to the safety of the device.

These include components such as:

\begin{itemize}

    \item power supply cord;

    \item input socket and fuse;

    \item relay;

    \item rocker switch;

    \item switching power supply;

    \item filter;

    \item internal electrical wiring;

    \item printed circuit board;

    \item auxiliary electrical components.

\end{itemize}

Potential risks associated with electrical component failure or abnormal
electrical conditions are addressed through the manufacturer's documented
risk-control measures.

\subsubsection{Thermal Hazards}

The principal function of the \textbf{BN-100 LUX} is to provide controlled
radiant heat to newborn and infant patients.

Accordingly, excessive temperature, insufficient temperature control and
failure of the heating system are relevant hazards considered in the risk
management documentation.

The manufacturer identifies temperature monitoring, temperature control,
over-temperature protection and alarm functions as safety-related measures.

\subsubsection{Temperature Monitoring Hazards}

The device uses a skin temperature sensor to monitor the patient's
temperature.

The risk management documentation identifies failures affecting the skin
temperature sensor, including:

\begin{itemize}

    \item sensor short circuit;

    \item sensor open circuit;

    \item abnormal temperature measurement.

\end{itemize}

Corresponding alarm and monitoring functions are provided as documented
risk-control measures.

\subsubsection{Alarm-Related Hazards}

The manufacturer identifies alarm functions associated with safety-related
conditions of the device.

These include:

\begin{itemize}

    \item over-temperature alarm;

    \item temperature deviation alarm;

    \item skin temperature sensor short-circuit alarm;

    \item skin temperature sensor open-circuit alarm;

    \item power failure alarm;

    \item manual-mode alarm.

\end{itemize}

The alarm functions are intended to provide an indication of specified
abnormal or potentially hazardous conditions.

\subsubsection{Mechanical Hazards}

The risk analysis also considers mechanical characteristics of the device,
including the patient support structure and associated components.

The manufacturer specifies mechanical limits for components such as the
mattress support and tray.

These limits are relevant to the prevention of mechanical overload and
associated hazards during intended operation.

\subsubsection{Patient-Contact Hazards}

The manufacturer identifies components that may come into direct contact
with the patient, including:

\begin{itemize}

    \item cotton baby mattress;

    \item stainless-steel skin temperature sensor probe.

\end{itemize}

These components are considered within the manufacturer's risk management
documentation with regard to patient contact and associated biological
considerations.

\subsubsection{Manufacturing-Related Risks}

The available risk management documentation also considers risks associated
with manufacturing and assembly processes.

Critical manufacturing and assembly steps are considered in relation to
the potential effect of process failures on the safety and performance of
the finished device.

The corresponding controls are addressed within the manufacturer's
documented production and risk-management framework.

\subsubsection{Risk Analysis Approach}

The risk analysis considers the relationship between:

\begin{itemize}

    \item intended use;

    \item device functions;

    \item identified hazards;

    \item reasonably foreseeable hazardous situations;

    \item possible causes of hazardous situations;

    \item potential harm;

    \item implemented risk-control measures.

\end{itemize}

The available manufacturer Risk Management Report constitutes the primary
documentary basis for this analysis.

\subsubsection{Documentation Limitation}

The present section summarizes the risk analysis documented by the
manufacturer.

No new hazard, probability value, severity value or residual-risk
classification is introduced independently of the available manufacturer
risk management documentation.

Where detailed risk-analysis records are required, the original Risk
Management Report and its associated risk-analysis tables should be
provided as supporting technical documentation.
